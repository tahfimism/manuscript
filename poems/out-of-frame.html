Out of frame

close enough to map, far enough to never exist

It didn't start as anything important,
just a habit of noticing more than needed,
reading what wasn't offered,
fixing what was never asked.

I stayed close without arriving
close enough to map your days,
far enough to never exist inside them,
a presence that learned how to be useful
without ever being chosen.

Somewhere in that, I started admiring the distance itself,
the way you lived in a world I could observe but not enter,
and I convinced myself that staying outside
was still a kind of staying.

Time didn't move forward so much as repeat,
like a thought I kept rethinking until it lost its origin,
until I couldn't tell whether I was remembering you
or reconstructing the idea of having been near you.

And when I tried to separate truth from interpretation,
there was less left than I expected.

I think that's when I understood
I was never part of what I was trying to be,
only part of what it looked like from outside.

And even then, I stayed.
because leaving would have required
admitting I had been standing in a place
that never had space for me in the first place.

So I kept observing.
Kept refining.
Kept turning everything into something I could carry
without breaking it or myself too visibly.

And what remained wasn't clarity or chaos,
not even distance anymore
just a mind that had learned too well
how to understand everything it could never belong to.

tai khan
