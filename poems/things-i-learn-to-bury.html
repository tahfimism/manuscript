Things I Learn to Bury

oceans trapped beneath ice

They teach us usefulness.
Endurance.
How to survive embarrassment
without visibly bleeeding from it.

You learn eaarly
that grief is acceptable only in controlled quantities.
That tenderness must arrive disguised as humor,
or loyalty, or silence.
Especially silence.

So I became excellent at silence.
Excellent at smiling normally,
while entire emotional catastrophes unfolded beneath the surface.
Excellent at saying "it's fine"
with the precisio n of practiced self-erasure.

And sometimes I wonder
how many men have mistaken emotional suppression for maturity
simply because nobody ever handed them a safer language for pain.

There were nights
I wanted someone to hold my face gently
and say:
you do not always have to survive things alone.

But the desire itself felt humiliating.
Like begging for something small enough
that you should have outgrown needing it years ago.

And maybe that is the tragedy.
Not that I do not feel deeply.
That feel deeply in complete isolation.
Like oceans trapped beneath ice,
visible only through fractures.
